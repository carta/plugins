# Carta Portfolio Analytics

A skill in the `carta-investors` plugin that spins up a local web console for a firm's **portfolio-company analytics** — operating KPIs from Carta **Data Collection** (`COMPANY_FINANCIALS`): revenue, ARR, EBITDA, gross profit, headcount, and every other metric a company reports — plus valuation, returns, cap tables, risk and benchmarking from Fund Admin. Modeled on the `carta-fund-modeling` micro-app, but for portfolio-company analytics instead of fund returns.

## How it works
Same architecture as the fund-modeling app:
1. The **skill** (`SKILL.md`) fetches KPI data over the Carta MCP and writes `kpi.json` + `firms.json` to a local cache (`~/.cache/carta-portfolio-analytics-app/`).
2. `serve.py` (Python stdlib, localhost-only, token-gated) serves the JSON plus a **no-build React app** — `.jsx` is transpiled in the browser by a service worker. **The browser never calls Carta.**

## Getting around
The app opens on **Overview**. The left sidebar lists the five tabs in one flat list, in this order: Overview · Company KPIs · Insights · Reporting health · Custom formulas. The Company page has no sidebar row of its own; it opens when you click a company name, and the Company KPIs row highlights while it is open. On a narrow screen the sidebar becomes a horizontal scrolling strip in the same order.

**Currency control (global).** The top bar carries a currency button (showing the target code, e.g. `USD`) that applies **across every tab**. It does two things: **exclude** any currencies the portfolio reports in (their companies drop out of all Dollar-metric views), and **convert** to a single target currency using **FX rates you enter** — blank means a currency is left as reported, never guessed. It's a no-op until you exclude a currency or enter a rate. Valuations and returns aren't converted (they carry no per-point currency). The button only appears when the portfolio reports in more than one currency.

**Update data.** A button in the top bar / sidebar lists each dataset (Operating KPIs, Forecasts, Holdings & returns, Ownership & cap tables) with when it was last fetched, and refreshes one or all of them in the background; **Load new data** then rebuilds and reloads. When the in-app refresh can't run (no `claude` binary on the PATH, or a cache built without a firm id) the panel shows freshness read-only and you refresh from chat instead.

**Companies are keyed by Carta identity.** Each company in the dashboard is its Fund Admin entity link, so a company's KPIs, holdings and cap table line up even when Carta spells its name three different ways, and two different companies that share a name stay apart. KPIs and cap tables that report from a second Carta corporation linked to the same issuer, and entity links that share one corporation, all land on that one company. Each company also knows whether it is a Carta cap-table customer, a paper company your firm created, or a general-ledger-only issuer.

## Views
- **Overview** — the landing page: a flat, click-to-sort table of **every company**, one row each, built for a 30-second scan of the book. The default columns are EBITDA (with a sparkline and QoQ delta, when the firm reports it), MOIC, Value, Invested and Last responded. **Filters & columns** opens a two-pane panel where you add any column from the catalog — any KPI, any position metric (ownership %, deal IRR, cost, price per share…), the built-in health facts (Burn/mo, Runway, runway Band, Invested, Value, MOIC, Last responded), Fund, every Carta tag category, Favorite and Signals — give a metric column its own sparkline and a QoQ / YoY delta, drag to reorder, or Reset to the default stack. **New formula…** in the same panel opens the Formulas editor, and the saved formula becomes a column. Anything addable as a column is also **filterable**: a funnel on every data column header takes a number/date condition (<, ≤, =, ≥, >, between, with an "include blanks" option) or a category pick (OR within a category, AND across filters); active filters show as chips in a ribbon and persist per firm, while the Companies pick is session-only. Click a row's chevron to expand an inline **drawer** — Position (Invested, Current value, Price per share, MOIC, Sector) beside the same Cap table card the Company page uses; a firm without Fund Administration sees a note saying position data comes from there. An **Expand all / Collapse all** button in the heading opens or closes every visible company's detail drawer at once. Every row carries a **favorite star** and the company name drills into the Company page.
- **Metrics** — a pivot matrix: every company × KPI down the rows, the reporting timeline across the columns. KPIs are alphabetical by default but you can **arrange the row order** of your selected metrics. Filter by metric, fund, company, tags, **favorites only** and **signals** (companies tripping a rule). A **Companies** picker at the top of the ribbon narrows the grid to one or more companies (or back to **All companies**); it drives the same company filter the Filters pane and saved views use. A **Configure** button gathers the display options: period window (4 / 8 / 12 / all), **Quarter-ends only**, **date order** (newest first, the default, or oldest first), the **Forecast** switch, **full figures** ($380,000 vs $380K), and **show % change** (period-over-period, green/red, N/A when there's no prior value) on a **QoQ or YoY basis**; the button counts how many options are off their defaults. Each company block can be **collapsed** to a single row, or all at once from the header. Long free-text answers are clipped to a uniform column width and shown in full on hover, so a prose KPI never widens the grid. **Save the filter setup + metric order as a named view** and reload it any time; **export to CSV**. Companies tripping a Signals rule show that rule as a **tag beside the company name**. Apply **conditional formatting** per metric — a **color scale** (heatmap gradient across the KPI's range) or **color-code rules** (threshold → color, e.g. Net Income < 0 → red), just like Excel — on either the **value** or its **% change**, and for color scales normalized **across the portfolio** or **per company** (so the colors read as that company's own trend).

**History window.** On a large firm (25,000+ KPI data points) the skill asks up front whether to load the **last 3 years** (default) or **everything** — on a large portfolio the 3-year window can halve the fetch, while on a smaller one it saves under 12%, so smaller firms are never asked. A window is recorded in `kpi.json` as `source.since`, shown in the sidebar, and causes the Coverage tab to withhold "Started reporting" (a cutoff makes a truncated company look like a new one).

**Metrics: forecasts extend the timeline.** A **Forecast** on/off switch (shown only when the firm has forecast data, off by default) continues the grid past the last actual with the companies' **own submitted estimates**. Forecasts only fill periods *after the data date* — a reported actual is never replaced — and forecast cells are washed **light blue** and italicised, with the column headers tinted to match. The blue outranks conditional formatting, because knowing a figure is an estimate matters more than where it sits on a heatmap. Forecast columns are labelled `(forecast)` in the CSV export, so an estimate can't be mistaken for an actual in Excel. Monthly forecasters get the same quarterly roll-up as actuals.

- **Coverage** — the state of your reported data, one navigation row across three questions: **Completeness** (is the data all there? — the default, because it's what you check before trusting anything downstream), **Data quality** (is what's there correct?), or **What changed** (what moved this quarter?).
  - *Completeness* — leads with a summary strip of reporting-health counts (Companies, On time, Late, Badly overdue, Never reported), then group **by company** for a company × quarter grid (checkmark reported / gap / not-expected, filtered to all companies or to a reporting-health bucket such as *badly overdue* or *never reported*), or **by metric** for a heatmap of % reported per metric per quarter; pick the metrics (defaults to core KPIs — Revenue, EBITDA, Cash, Net Income, Gross Profit, Headcount — filled out with whatever else is available) and a 4 / 8 / 12 / all quarter window. Each company is judged against **its own cadence** (an annual reporter isn't late at six months). The newest quarter is hidden by default while it's still filing — an **Include in-progress quarter** toggle brings it back — and a quarter otherwise counts as a **gap** only after a company has *started* reporting that metric, so pre-investment periods don't create false gaps while a company that goes dark surfaces trailing gaps. **Export a chase list** (by company) or the **gap list** (by metric) to CSV.
  - *Data quality* — internal contradictions in what companies submitted: balance sheets that don't balance, Revenue − COGS ≠ Gross Profit, impossible values, 10x jumps — with a 1% tolerance so rounding isn't flagged. **None of it indicates a Carta error.**
  - *What changed* — biggest movers, ranked **by size of change** or **by %** (size is the default, since a % swing on a tiny base is noise; both figures are shown), who **went dark**, who just started reporting, and current signal breaches. It defaults to the most recent quarter whose reporting has **settled**, because a quarter that just closed is always under-reported — defaulting to the newest one would invent a wave of false "went dark" alerts; pick it anyway and the view says so.
- **Company** (360) — the deep-dive page for one company, and the drill-through target for every clickable company name in the app. It is organised in three tabs — **Summary** (risk & signals, KPIs with portfolio rank and reporting health, qualitative KPIs, returns, valuation, credit), **Cap table** (the cap table with its stack, rights and holdings, plus SOI performance) and **Forecast** (every forecast mode). **Notes** sits under every tab. Heavy detail on the Summary tab (the full KPI grid, the peer breakdown) is condensed by default and expands in place. The cards:
  - *Qualitative KPIs* — the company's flag / date / free-text KPIs (see Qualitative KPIs in `SKILL.md`), directly under the KPI snapshot.
  - *Investment returns (gross)* — cost, gross MOIC, deal IRR, unrealized, holding period, stamped with the data's as-of date.
  - *Valuation & last round* — valuation multiple, last round, mark vs round.
  - *SOI performance* — the fund's mark on this company over time as a line chart, switchable between **Value per share** and **Position value** (FMV vs cost). Total value adds cumulative proceeds, so a realized exit steps up to the cash returned instead of crashing to zero; the post-exit area is hatched as realized cash and ticked at each distribution.
  - *KPI snapshot* — the headline KPIs, one row each: the latest filed figure, its change, and the company's **rank and percentile in the portfolio** on that KPI (hover for the portfolio median). A **Latest / Since invested** switch sets what change and rank measure: the latest filing against the one before it, or against the first figure reported after the fund invested — in which case rank orders the portfolio by that same change. Since invested needs an investment date, so it is disabled for a firm without Fund Admin holdings. A **status line** above the table gives reporting health: on time or late against the company's own cadence, when it last filed, how many metrics, and chips counting missing quarters and internal contradictions (hover for the detail). The card stays on the page for a company that reports no KPIs at all, with a **Never reported** badge and a plain no-data note, so the reporting status is always one glance away. **Configure KPIs** picks which metrics appear, for **this company** or for **all companies**; a dot marks an override and Reset clears it.
  - *Cap table* — a one-line **firm ownership** summary (fully-diluted, summed across the funds that hold the company, with **ownership by fund**) above the cap table by security class, rights & preferences with the liquidation stack, and your holdings line by line, on the Cap table tab. **This is where cap tables live** — there is no separate top-level tab, since it rendered the very same panels.
  - *Forecast* — the Forecast tab, always present. Actuals vs the company's latest-logged forecast by default, with the forecast revisions (a vintage × period matrix) beneath the chart on the same KPI, switchable to auto-forecast — a projection from the company's own actuals with a selectable method and ±1σ band, plus, where the company has logged its own forecast, a bias-adjusted line (that forecast discounted by its historical bias, weight slider) and a status line scoring it (MAPE, bias, forecasts scored, rank among peers). A company with no logged forecast is told so in Actuals vs forecast and keeps the Auto-forecast tab.
  - *Credit & leverage* — the five leverage ratios and the inputs behind them, with a warning when negative EBITDA makes a ratio meaningless rather than passing, plus this company's covenant tests.
  - *Risk & signals* — the built-in and custom Signals rules this company trips.
  - *Notes* — a persisted free-text box.

  **Every card stays on the page** — a card whose data the firm lacks says what is missing and where it would come from (Fund Admin holdings for returns and SOI history, the Carta cap table for share classes), so a Data-Collection-only firm sees the same three tabs as a fund-admin firm. Qualitative KPIs is the one exception: a firm with no flag, date or text KPIs anywhere never sees it. KPI figures are shown **as filed**; the full history grid behind **View all KPIs** repeats the reporting status line. **Download PDF** prints the page as a **tearsheet** — the app's controls drop away and a title block with the firm name, company and data date takes their place. A company page is a **linkable URL** (`/firm/<slug>/company/<company>[/<tab>]`); the optional tab segment opens that tab, so you can send someone to exactly what you're looking at, and a link to a card from an older version opens the tab that holds it.
- **Portfolio** — the whole-book views, switched with one control: a growth × value **Quadrant** (bubble = a third metric), a rule-based **Signals** watchlist, and **Benchmarks** (below). The one built-in Signals rule flags a sustained revenue decline; on top of it you can add your **own custom rules** — pick a metric, a measure (latest value / QoQ growth % / YoY growth % / % vs forecast), an operator, and a threshold (e.g. *Runway < 12* or *Revenue QoQ growth < 10%*), like conditional formatting in Excel. Rules save locally and also surface on each Company page and as an Overview column and filter. The built-in set can be toggled off to run a fully custom watchlist.
  - A **bubble-size key** under the chart shows three reference bubbles with their values, and the picker is labelled **Bubble size**.
  - **Benchmarks** — pick a metric and see the portfolio **median + quartile spread** in a stat bar, then a **distribution strip** per cohort: a shaded band marks the **middle 50% (P25–P75)** with labeled P25 / median / P75 markers, and every company is a dot over it (green above / red below the cohort median, click to open). A **group-by** control re-buckets the cohort: whole *Portfolio*, *Stage* (Seed / Series A / B …), *Vintage* (investment year), or *Valuation band* (post-money); non-standard rounds fold into an *Other* lane. A companion table gives each company's value, % vs median, percentile, and quartile. Grouped cohorts need the matching Carta cap-table / returns input.
- **Formulas** — one list of your **custom metrics** and, on a second switch, your **covenants**.
  - *Custom metrics* — build a metric from arithmetic on KPIs with an insert-KPI + operator builder, live preview, and per-company results + time-series; choose the result type ($ / % / × / number). **One-click presets** start you off: **Rule of 40** (`yoy({Revenue}) + {EBITDA} ÷ {Revenue}` — revenue growth plus EBITDA margin), gross margin, revenue per employee, and runway; a preset only appears when the firm actually reports its inputs. Two time functions are available — `yoy(…)` (change vs ~a year earlier) and `prior(…)` (the value a year earlier) — both matched on the **calendar** (~12 months, ±2), never a fixed number of reported periods, so they mean the same thing for a monthly reporter as a quarterly one. **Assign to companies** scopes a metric to some or all companies and lets any company carry its **own roll-up expression** when its accounts differ from the default, with each row showing the value it resolves to (or the missing input). **Rule of 40 is seeded automatically** on a firm's first load (only when that firm reports the KPIs it needs) — delete it and it stays deleted. Saved locally. **Saved formulas become first-class derived KPIs** — they flow into the Overview table (as columns and filters), the Metrics pivot (marked ƒ), Company page charts, and the Signals rule builder. In the Overview table a formula can also reference position facts (MOIC, invested, value…) and the computed Burn/mo and Runway as inputs.
  - *Covenants* — the same builder plus a **threshold and a test** — the PE/private-credit workflow. Set whether the value must stay at most or at least some number and the app tests it every quarter, reporting **headroom** (how much room is left before it trips), a **Watch** band for early warning, the **trend** in headroom, and a portfolio table sorted tightest-first. Tested on an **LTM** basis by default the way credit agreements define maintenance tests — flows summed over four quarters, balances at closing — with a per-covenant point-in-time option, and **dated step-down schedules** so each period is judged by the threshold actually in force then. A **meaningfulness guard** stops the classic false pass: Debt ÷ *negative* EBITDA is a negative ratio that trivially satisfies "at most 4x", so those quarters are flagged **Not meaningful** rather than passed. Presets cover leverage, net leverage, liquidity, minimum EBITDA, current ratio and debt/ARR — and interest coverage, fixed-charge coverage, DSCR and capex limits ship built in, appearing automatically for any firm that reports interest expense, capex or scheduled principal. This tests reported KPI data against thresholds you enter; it does not read credit agreements.

### Favorites
A one-click **star** marks the companies you watch most. It sits on every Overview row, on the Metrics rows and in the Company page header, and one click flips it everywhere. On a firm's first load the **top quartile by cost basis** is pre-starred so the list isn't empty on day one; that seed runs exactly once, so an auto-pick you un-star never comes back and your own stars are never overridden. Favorites are a Metrics filter (**favorites only**) and an Overview column and filter, and they save locally with your other app state.

### Tags
Companies carry two kinds of tag, and the app keeps them visibly apart.

**From Carta** — the investment tags on your Fund Admin holdings, in whatever categories your firm uses: Industry, Country, Geography, and any other. *Every* category is surfaced, not a fixed pair. These are **read-only** — Carta exposes no write path for them, so the app never pretends it can save an edit back; change them in Carta and re-run the fetch. In dense rows the Status chip stays visible and the rest collapse behind a **"Tags (N)"** pill that expands in place.

**Status** — a computed category, listed first, splitting the portfolio into **Active**, **Partially realized**, **Realized** and **Written off**. Carta reports no status flag, so it is derived from each position's own economics: remaining value says what is still held, proceeds say what has come back.

| Remaining value | Proceeds | Status | Chip |
|---|---|---|---|
| > 0 | 0 | Active | green |
| > 0 | > 0 | Partially realized | amber |
| 0 | > 0 | Realized | dark |
| 0 | 0 (with a cost basis) | Written off | red |

The colours are the design system's semantic tones, so they follow the Ink tokens into dark mode. Realized is deliberately *not* blue: blue is what every other Carta category wears, and a status in that colour would read as just another category. Colour is never the only signal — the chip always carries its label.

It filters like any other tag, so "Realized" alone, or "Realized + Healthcare" together, both work. A company with no holdings row gets **no** status rather than a guessed one — *unknown* and *active* are different claims, and only one is honest. Firms with KPI coverage but no Fund Admin holdings therefore see no Status filter at all.

**Your own** — free-form labels you add in the app ("Board seat", "Follow-on candidate"). Add them from a Metrics row or the Company page: `+ tag`, type, Enter. Tags already in use elsewhere are offered for one-click reuse, so a set doesn't rot into *Board Seat* and *board seat*. They save **locally**, in the same file as your notes, saved views and Signals rules, and are never sent to Carta.

Both kinds drive the Metrics tab's **Tags** filter and appear as Overview columns and filters. Picking two values inside one category widens the list (Industry: CleanTech *or* BioTech); picking across categories narrows it (CleanTech *and* Canada). A tag filter is captured in a **saved view** and noted in the CSV filename, so a filtered export can't be mistaken for the whole portfolio.

> Carta tag coverage tracks Fund Admin holdings. A firm with holdings is usually tagged almost completely; a Data-Collection-only or cap-table-only firm has **no** Carta tags at all, and the filter says so rather than showing an empty menu. Your own tags work for every firm either way.

Every chart (line / bar / area and the portfolio quadrant) carries a small toolbar with an **Export ▾** dropdown: **download the chart** as **PNG** or **SVG** (CSS colors are resolved so the file renders correctly outside the app). The Company page's KPI chart wall follows Ink's own chart recipe (dot-matrix background, a headline value leading each card, a zero-anchored axis) rather than this shared toolbar. Period-over-period change (QoQ / YoY) shows **N/A** when there's no prior value to compare against.

Views that need special inputs (cap-table valuation, investment date, round stage, a logged forecast) stay **honest about coverage**: any company that can't be shown is called out by name in a **"N companies not shown — <reason>"** note beneath the view (Valuation, Leverage, Benchmarks, and Actuals-vs-Forecast), so a shorter table always says *who* was excluded and *why* rather than silently dropping companies.

### Monthly → quarterly roll-up (flows vs balances)
On the quarter grid, a company that reports **monthly** is rolled up to the quarter — and *how* depends on what the metric is. The source `REPORT_TYPE` drives it, captured at build time as each metric's `agg`:
- **Flows are summed** — *Profit and loss* (Revenue, COGS, Gross Profit, EBITDA, Net Income, Operating Income, Total Opex) and *Cash flow* movements (Operating / Investing / Financing, Change in Cash). A monthly reporter's Q2 revenue is Apr + May + Jun, not just June.
- **Balances take the closing value** — the whole *Balance sheet*, and critically **Period-End Cash**, which sits on the cash-flow statement but is a closing balance and must **never** be summed. Unclassified *KPI* rows (headcount, runway, custom metrics) also default to point-in-time, since summing a level would invent a figure that never existed.
- Hover any quarter cell to see how it was built ("Sum of 3 reported periods: …" / "Closing value of 3 reported periods: …").
- A red **\*** marks a **partial quarter** — a summed flow where the company filed only some of the months, so the total is understated. (Balances aren't flagged: a closing value is valid whichever month it came from.) The Coverage tab's Completeness view shows exactly which months are missing.
- The roll-up applies to the **Metrics pivot**. On the quarterly basis, **QoQ and YoY compare whole quarters** (Q4 total vs Q3 total), not one month against another three months earlier. Company page shows KPI figures **as filed**, so a monthly reporter's row is its latest month and the change is against its previous filing.
- Whether a quarter looks "partial" is judged **against the preceding quarter**, not the company's lifetime cadence — so a company that filed 3 months in Q3 and only 1 in Q4 is flagged, while a company that simply switched from quarterly to monthly reporting is not falsely flagged.

### Reporting frequency (monthly vs quarterly)
Companies report KPIs at different cadences — some monthly, some quarterly, some annually — and the tool accounts for this end-to-end:
- **QoQ / YoY are calendar-based**, not slot-based: QoQ compares to the value **~3 months** back and YoY to **~12 months** back (within a tolerance), so "YoY" is a true year regardless of how often a company reports. (A naive "4 data points back" would make a monthly reporter's "YoY" only 4 months.) This flows through Compare, Company page, the Portfolio quadrant, custom Signals rules, and the Metrics tab's % change toggle. Hover any QoQ / YoY figure to see the exact prior period used.
- Each company's **cadence is shown as a "Freq" tag** (Monthly / Quarterly / Annual) on Company page.
- When companies report the **same metric at different cadences**, a **"Mixed reporting frequency"** note appears — the % changes are still comparable, but it flags that raw *levels* of a flow metric (e.g. revenue) aren't directly comparable across a monthly vs a quarterly reporter.
- The Metrics tab's **"Quarter-ends only"** toggle (on by default) aligns everyone onto a common quarterly grid; the Company page's forecast card detects each company's cadence and projects at the right spacing.

## Use it
This ships as part of the `carta-investors` plugin — no separate install. You need the Carta MCP connector enabled in your Claude Code session, `uv` installed, and access in Carta to the Investment Firm Account you want to look at. The app pulls data live using *your* Carta permissions, so you only see firms you're authorized for.

Just ask Claude to open the dashboard. **If you don't name a firm, Claude prompts you for an Investment Firm Account first** (firm name or Carta firm URL) — nothing runs until you pick one. You can also name it up front: **"portfolio analytics for Acme Ventures"**. A warm cache reopens with no MCP call. Inside the app, the **Update data** button shows how fresh each dataset is and refreshes one dataset or all of them in the background; you can also just say "refresh KPI data".

> KPI coverage is partial — only portfolio companies that report into Carta Data Collection appear. Firms with no coverage open to a clean empty state. Returns, cap tables and the SOI performance chart need Fund Administration data; the app hides what a firm doesn't have rather than showing empty panels.
